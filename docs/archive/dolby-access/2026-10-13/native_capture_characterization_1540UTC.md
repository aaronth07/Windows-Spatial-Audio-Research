# Offline characterization of the completed native test

The property-dependent effect exceeds a shared gain or the common capture-latency shift. Captured spectral changes also vary by direction and ear. This supports a native rendering-tuning effect; it does not independently establish listener physiology, improvement, direct internal deserialization, or use of every coefficient.

The reproducible script is `scripts/characterize_native_captures.py`; its inputs are the exact six manifest-hashed WAVs from `transaction_20261013T150234101Z`. It uses candidate2 versus baseline1 without a timing shift, and checks candidate1 using only the reviewed shared stereo −122-sample shift. Both candidate arrays and all controls match exactly across 504,000 aligned/zero-padded frames. The shift discards no nonzero samples. Channel mask 3 establishes the recorded left/right order. No gain or time adjustment is applied separately to either ear.

Full raw partitions cover every retained sample, with edges 0, 96,000, 192,000, 288,000, 384,000 and 504,000. They use no taper or response normalization. The global scalar fit is 0.937197 (−0.563 dB), leaving 29.831% relative L2 residual against the candidate. Direction-specific shared gains leave 20.316–35.063% residual. Baseline global peak is 0.00326538 in each ear; candidate peaks are 0.00393677/0.00396729. Raw RMS and energy for every capture/ear are recorded in the JSON; capture lengths differ only in silent tail extent.

All capture amplitudes lie on a PCM16 grid despite the float WAV format. The first 450 ms before the impulse contain identical baseline/candidate background: RMS about 1.43e−5, with peak 1/32768. Full-partition tails also contain this one-step background. Applying a common filter to the whole raw signal therefore also transforms a background that is unchanged by the profile; its residual cannot be attributed entirely to directional coefficients.

On the full raw FFT grid, a common complex frequency response across all ten direction/ear responses leaves 8.197%, 11.251% and 16.084% residual at relative magnitude floors −20/−30/−40 dB. The corresponding all-ten supported-bin counts are 3,579/9,646/35,139 of 54,559 bins from 20–20,000 Hz. Additional quiet-RMS thresholds use multiplier × quietRMS × sqrt(partitionFrames), under a stationary broadband-background assumption. At multiplier 3, all-ten support falls to 1,026 bins and residual is 5.630%. Using available supported ears in at least two distinct directions gives 1,989 bins and 6.037% residual. These thresholds are sensitivity checks, not statistical significance bounds.

The separately labeled optional early-response analysis uses a shared 28 ms rectangle, from 8 ms before through 20 ms after the first baseline impulse peak, repeated at the fixed two-second spacing. It retains 100%, 98.315%, 98.464%, 93.998% and 97.722% of paired difference energy for front/left/right/rear/above. It includes early reflections and is not an isolated direct HRTF. The full raw results remain preserved. At the stricter quiet threshold, the common-filter fit over 787 of 853 bins supported in at least two directions leaves 19.925% residual. Requiring all ten responses leaves only 16 bins and a 21.963% residual; weak far-ear bins are explicitly excluded. Both outcomes and support sensitivity are retained, avoiding division near weak/null responses.

Raw right-versus-left correlation peaks remain at integer lag zero; interpolated changes are below one sample (20.833 microseconds). Full raw low-frequency phase slopes yield inconsistent multi-millisecond delays and fail the recorded phase-linearity/+/-1 ms screen. Early-response phase fits for left/right are approximately +/-0.65 ms, with candidate changes −10.897/−1.878 microseconds, but sparse support, quantization, estimator disagreement and reflection/truncation effects prevent a resolved physiological ITD-change claim. Interaural level/phase spectra and estimator details are provided directly in the data.

Artifacts are in `private/native_load_validation/transaction_20261013T150234101Z/offline_characterization/`: `characterization.json`, full-bin NPZ, frequency and direction CSVs, raw PNG/SVG plots, and separate optional early-response JSON/CSV/NPZ/PNG/SVG. CSV decimation is recorded; calculations use the complete FFT grids. Frequency ticks use ordinary numeric Hz. The plots were visually inspected and tick overlap corrected. Input and artifact hashes are independently rechecked. Synthetic checks distinguish pure gain, common EQ, common delay, direction-specific change and ear delay; they also cover unsupported/noisy bins and alignment that would lose nonzero data.
